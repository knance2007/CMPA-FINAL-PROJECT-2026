This retrospective reflects on my project process using the three key questions from PMUPM Chapter 7: what went well, what went wrong, and what I would do differently. This project involved building a two‑page fictional law firm website, along with supporting documents such as a scope statement, project plan, and this retrospective.

### What Went Well
One of the strongest and best parts of this project was how quickly the structure came together once I defined the scope. Creating the home page and about page early on gave me a clear foundation to build from, and sticking to semantic HTML helped keep everything organized, user friendly, and readable.

The visual elements also came together very nicely. I decided to go with the banner on the home page and the three‑image layout on the About page to make the site feel more polished and intentional.  The image alignment gave the layout a clean, modern feel.

Writing the scope statement, plan, and retrospective while buiding my site helped me stay aligned with my goals and avoid adding in features that were outside the scope of my assignment. This is something PMUPM emphasizes, and I could feel how much smoother the project went because of it.

### What Went Wrong
Most of the issues I ran into were small but surprisingly time‑consuming. Image paths caused more trouble than I expected,  especially dealing with capitalization and filenames with spaces. These weren’t major problems, but they slowed me down and reminded me how sensitive web development can be to tiny details.

I also underestimated how long it would take to fine‑tune spacing, sizing, and layout.  I found myself adjusting widths, margins, font sizing and color more than I planned. It wasn’t “wrong,” but it did highlight that design work often requires more patience than expected.

### What I’d Do Differently
If I were starting this project again, I would set up a more consistent file‑naming system from the beginning. Using lowercase letters, avoiding spaces, and keeping extensions predictable would have prevented most of the image issues I ran into.

From a project‑management point of view, I’d break my tasks into smaller, more trackable pieces. Though I did follow my plan pretty closely, I would allow myself some grace when working on certain areas of the project and give myself more time in other areas of the project.   I’ll carry that forward into future coursework and projects.
